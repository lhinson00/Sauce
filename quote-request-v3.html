Roof / Truss Type

The truss is the framework that supports your roof. This choice affects your building's look, overhang options, and maximum door sizes.
🪵 Wood Truss
Traditional look. Roof can be flush at the wall or extend out with a finished overhang (soffit). More headroom for garage doors.
🔩 Steel Truss
Open web design. Roof can be flush or have an 18" overhang (no finished soffit). Required for partially enclosed and roof-only buildings.
Roof Overhang
The overhang is how far the roof extends past the wall. A finished soffit covers the underside for a clean, polished look. On wood truss buildings, soffit applies to both gable ends and eave (side) walls.
Flush
Roof meets the wall with no overhang
12" Overhang
Gable ends + eave walls, with finished soffit
18" Overhang
Gable ends + eave walls, with finished soffit
Roof Overhang (Eave Walls Only)
Steel truss overhang is available on partially enclosed and roof-only buildings only, and applies to the eave (side) walls. No finished soffit with steel.
Flush
Roof meets the wall — no overhang
18" Overhang
Eave walls only, no finished soffit

Post Spacing / Bay Size
Posts are the vertical columns that hold up the roof. The space between posts is called a "bay." Standard bays are 10' or 12' apart. Need to drive equipment between posts? Wider bays give you more room, but require heavier framing.
Standard (10' or 12')
Wide (14-16')
Extra wide (18-20')
Not sure — let's discuss
⚠️ Heads up on wider bays: Openings wider than 12' use a steel support beam (header) across the top, which lowers the drive-through clearance in those bays by about 18 inches. So a 14' tall building with wide bays gives you roughly 12' 6" of clearance to drive through. We'll show the exact clearance on your quote.
